## Why the Script Is Everything

Here's a counterintuitive truth about **video ad scripts**: the script matters more than the production quality. Way more.

You can have a $50,000 production budget with cinematic lighting and professional actors, but if the script is generic, the ad will flop. Conversely, a well-scripted ad filmed on an iPhone can outperform it 10x.

Why? Because in the first 2-3 seconds, nobody notices your production quality. They notice your *message*. The hook either resonates or it doesn't. The promise either intrigues or it doesn't. And once you've lost them, no amount of B-roll will bring them back.

The script controls:

- **Whether people watch** (the hook)
- **Whether they stay engaged** (the story/problem)
- **Whether they want the product** (the solution/proof)
- **Whether they take action** (the CTA)

Master **video ad copywriting**, and you've mastered the highest-leverage skill in digital marketing.

## Anatomy of a High-Converting Video Ad Script

Every great **ad script** has five parts. Think of them as acts in a mini-play:

### Act 1: The Hook (0-3 seconds)

The hook is the gatekeeper. If it fails, the rest of the script is irrelevant — nobody will hear it. Your hook should do one of three things:

- **Create curiosity:** Make them think "wait, what?"
- **Call out the audience:** Make them think "that's me!"
- **Promise value:** Make them think "I need to know this"

### Act 2: The Problem (3-10 seconds)

Describe the viewer's pain point so accurately they feel seen. Specificity is key — "struggling with acne" is weak; "tired of trying expensive serums that just make your skin oilier" is strong.

### Act 3: The Bridge (10-20 seconds)

This is the transition from problem to solution. It's the "and then I discovered..." moment. The bridge should feel natural and conversational, building anticipation for the product reveal.

### Act 4: The Proof (20-40 seconds)

This is where you sell — but through evidence, not hype. Share specific benefits, results, features that surprised you, or social proof. "Over 10,000 people switched last month" hits different than "our product is popular."

### Act 5: The CTA (Last 5-10 seconds)

Tell them exactly what to do, and give them a reason to do it now. Urgency, scarcity, or a simple direct instruction all work.

## 15 Hook Formulas That Stop the Scroll

The hook is where most ads fail. Here are 15 proven formulas you can adapt for any product:

### Curiosity Hooks

1. **"I can't believe nobody talks about this..."** — Creates fear of missing out on insider knowledge.
2. **"This [product] does something I've never seen before..."** — Promises novelty.
3. **"I was today years old when I learned..."** — Familiar meme format that triggers curiosity.

### Problem-Calling Hooks

4. **"If you [specific problem], you NEED to hear this..."** — Directly calls out the target audience.
5. **"Stop wasting money on [common solution that doesn't work]..."** — Challenges existing behavior.
6. **"I tried everything for [problem] — this is the only thing that worked."** — Signals you've done the research for them.

### Bold Claim Hooks

7. **"This ₹999 product replaced my ₹15,000 [routine/tool/service]."** — Value comparison that creates instant interest.
8. **"I've tested 20+ [product category] and this is the only one I'd recommend."** — Authority through experience.
9. **"[X] in 5 minutes instead of [Y] hours? Let me show you."** — Time-saving promise.

### Social Proof Hooks

10. **"There's a reason this has 50,000+ five-star reviews..."** — Leverages crowd wisdom.
11. **"My [friend/dermatologist/trainer] told me about this and wow..."** — Third-party recommendation.
12. **"This went viral for a reason — and it's not hype."** — Taps into FOMO.

### Direct/Confrontational Hooks

13. **"Stop scrolling. If you [do X], this will change your life."** — Pattern interrupt.
14. **"Unpopular opinion: [commonly accepted thing] is a waste of money."** — Controversy creates engagement.
15. **"If you're still [doing old thing], you're leaving money on the table."** — Fear of missing out.

## 4 Proven Script Frameworks

### 1. PAS — Problem, Agitate, Solution

The most versatile framework. Works for almost any product.

- **Problem:** Identify the pain point
- **Agitate:** Make it feel worse — poke the wound
- **Solution:** Present your product as the relief

*Example: "Are you spending hours editing product videos? (Problem) Between hiring freelancers, waiting for revisions, and spending thousands per video, it's exhausting. (Agitate) Zockto creates studio-quality AI video ads in 5 minutes flat — starting at ₹999/month. (Solution)"*

### 2. AIDA — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action

Classic marketing framework adapted for video.

- **Attention:** Hook that grabs focus
- **Interest:** Share something intriguing about the product
- **Desire:** Paint a picture of life with the product
- **Action:** Clear CTA

### 3. BAB — Before, After, Bridge

Perfect for transformation-based products.

- **Before:** Life before the product (the struggle)
- **After:** Life after (the transformation)
- **Bridge:** The product that makes the transformation possible

### 4. The 3-Step — Hook, Story, Offer

The simplest framework, perfect for UGC-style ads.

- **Hook:** 2-3 seconds to grab attention
- **Story:** Your experience with the product (authentic, relatable)
- **Offer:** What the viewer gets and how to get it

## Word Counts by Video Length

People speak at approximately 130-150 words per minute in a natural, conversational pace. Here's how that translates:

| Video Length | Word Count | Best For |
|-------------|-----------|----------|
| 15 seconds | 35-45 words | TikTok, Reels, bumper ads |
| 30 seconds | 70-90 words | Facebook feed, Instagram, standard ads |
| 45 seconds | 105-130 words | Detailed product demos, testimonials |
| 60 seconds | 140-170 words | YouTube pre-rolls, story-driven ads |

**Pro tip:** Always read your script out loud and time it. If it feels rushed, cut words. If it drags, tighten the pacing. Natural delivery should feel unhurried but not slow.

## The Psychology Behind Scripts That Sell

Understanding *why* certain scripts work helps you write better ones. Here are the psychological principles at play:

### Loss Aversion

People feel losses twice as strongly as equivalent gains. "Stop wasting ₹5,000/month on [old solution]" hits harder than "Save ₹5,000/month with [new solution]." Frame your script around what they're losing by NOT using your product.

### Social Proof

"10,000 people switched last month" works because we look to others' behavior when we're uncertain. Mention numbers — reviews, customers, units sold — whenever you can.

### Specificity = Credibility

"Improved my skin in 14 days" is more believable than "improves your skin fast." Specific claims feel measured and honest. Vague claims feel like marketing fluff.

### The Curiosity Gap

When you know part of something but not all of it, your brain craves the missing piece. Hooks like "I can't believe nobody talks about this..." create a gap that can only be closed by watching the rest of the video.

### Authority Bias

"Recommended by dermatologists" or "Used by 500+ brands" adds credibility through perceived authority. Even "I've tested 20 products and this is #1" establishes personal authority through experience.

## 7 Script Mistakes That Kill Conversions

### 1. Starting with Your Brand Name

"Hi, I'm [Name] from [Brand]!" — SKIP. Nobody watches ads for introductions. Lead with the hook, introduce yourself (if at all) after you've earned their attention.

### 2. Feature Dumping

Listing every feature is boring. Instead of "our product has 48 languages, 150 avatars, 200 voices, 100 templates, and 1080p output," say "imagine creating a professional video ad in 5 minutes, in any language, with zero editing skills." Benefits > features, always.

### 3. Being Too Vague

"Our product is amazing and will change your life" tells the viewer nothing. What specifically does it do? What specifically changes? Vague scripts produce vague results.

### 4. Sounding Like a Script

If your script uses words like "revolutionary," "state-of-the-art," "cutting-edge," or "introducing our premium solution" — rewrite it. Talk like a human talking to another human.

### 5. Forgetting the Visual Layer

Your script shouldn't describe everything verbally. What can be shown visually? Product in action, results on screen, before/after images — use the visual medium. The script should complement the visuals, not replace them.

### 6. Weak or Missing CTA

"Check us out" is weak. "Tap the link below — they're running 30% off for the next 48 hours and I grabbed mine before it sold out" is strong. Be specific, add urgency, make it easy.

### 7. Writing for 60 Seconds When You Should Write for 15

Match your script length to your platform and objective. Top-of-funnel awareness ads work better short (15s). Retargeting ads where people already know you can be longer (30-60s). Not every message needs a minute.

## How to A/B Test Your Scripts

The final — and most important — piece of the puzzle:

### Test One Variable at a Time

If you change the hook, avatar, and CTA between two ads, you won't know what caused the performance difference. Test one variable per comparison:

- **Round 1:** Test 5 different hooks (same body and CTA)
- **Round 2:** Take the winning hook, test 3 different body structures
- **Round 3:** Take the winning body, test 3 different CTAs

### Budget Per Variation

₹500-1,000 per variation is enough for initial signal on Meta. You need roughly 1,000-2,000 impressions per variation to see meaningful differences in CTR and engagement. Pause losers after 48-72 hours.

### What Metrics to Watch

- **Hook rate:** What % of viewers watch past 3 seconds? This measures your hook effectiveness.
- **Hold rate:** What % watch past 50%? This measures your body content.
- **CTR:** Who clicks? This measures your overall persuasion + CTA.
- **Conversion rate:** Who buys? The ultimate metric.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do you write a script for a video ad?**
A: Write a video ad script in 5 parts: 1) Hook — grab attention in the first 2-3 seconds with a bold claim, question, or relatable statement. 2) Problem — identify the viewer's pain point. 3) Solution — introduce your product naturally. 4) Proof — share benefits, results, or social proof. 5) CTA — tell the viewer exactly what to do next. Keep language conversational, focus on benefits over features, and always read the script aloud before finalizing.

**Q: How long should a video ad script be?**
A: Script length depends on video duration. For a 15-second ad: 35-45 words. For a 30-second ad: 70-90 words. For a 60-second ad: 140-170 words. People speak at roughly 130-150 words per minute in a natural, conversational pace. Always time yourself reading the script aloud to check pacing.

**Q: What makes a good hook for a video ad?**
A: A good video ad hook does one of these: asks a provocative question, makes a bold claim, calls out a specific audience, presents a surprising fact, or creates curiosity. Examples: "Stop buying [product category] until you see this", "I was today years old when I learned...", "This ₹999 product replaced my ₹15,000 routine". The hook should be 2-3 seconds and make viewers feel they'll miss out by scrolling past.

**Q: What's the best script framework for video ads?**
A: The most effective frameworks for video ads are: PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution) for pain-point driven products, AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action) for general products, BAB (Before-After-Bridge) for transformation products, and the 3-Step (Hook-Story-Offer) for UGC-style ads. PAS is the most versatile and works well for beginners.
