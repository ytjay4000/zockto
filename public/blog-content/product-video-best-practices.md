## Tip 1: Nail the Hook in Under 3 Seconds

This is the single most important product video tip you'll ever learn. **You have less than 3 seconds to stop the scroll.** That's it. If your video opens with a logo animation or a slow zoom on your product, you've already lost most of your audience.

What works instead:

- **Pain-point callouts:** "Tired of [specific problem]?" — immediately signals relevance to the viewer.
- **Bold claims:** "This tiny gadget replaced my entire morning routine" — curiosity is irresistible.
- **Pattern interrupts:** Start with unexpected visuals or a statement that makes people do a double-take.
- **Direct address:** "If you're a [target audience], watch this" — self-selection hooks work because they feel personal.

The hook isn't just the first frame. It's the first *moment* — visual + audio combined. Your opening needs to earn the next 10 seconds of attention.

> **💡 Pro Tip:** Create 3-5 different hooks for the same product video and A/B test them. The hook alone can swing your click-through rate by 50-200%. With AI tools, generating hook variations is trivially cheap — just swap the opening line and regenerate.

## Tip 2: Lead with Benefits, Not Features

This is Marketing 101, but it's astonishing how many product videos get it wrong. Nobody cares that your blender has a "1200W motor with 6 titanium blades." They care that it makes perfect smoothies in 30 seconds with zero chunks.

**Features tell. Benefits sell.**

Here's the mental framework: for every feature, ask "so what?" until you hit an emotional benefit.

- "1200W motor" → "so what?" → "blends anything in seconds" → "so what?" → **"Your morning smoothie is ready before your coffee brews"**
- "Waterproof to 50m" → "so what?" → "works in the shower, pool, ocean" → "so what?" → **"Never worry about taking it off — it goes wherever you go"**

Your product video script should be 80% benefits, 20% features. Lead with how the product changes someone's life, then sprinkle in the specs for the detail-oriented buyers.

## Tip 3: Match Video Length to the Platform

There's no universal "perfect" video length. The right duration depends entirely on where the video will run:

- **TikTok / Instagram Reels:** 15-30 seconds. Fast pace, punchy edits, one clear message.
- **Facebook / Instagram Feed:** 30-60 seconds. Room for storytelling and social proof. This is where longer formats shine.
- **YouTube (pre-roll / shorts):** 15 seconds for skippable pre-roll hooks, 30-60 seconds for YouTube Shorts.
- **Product Pages:** 60-90 seconds. Buyers are already interested — give them the full picture.
- **Email / WhatsApp:** 15-30 seconds. Quick, punchy, one CTA.

## Tip 5: Show the Product in Action

Don't just *talk* about your product. **Show it doing its thing.**

This sounds obvious, but too many product videos are just talking heads or spec lists without ever demonstrating the product in a real-world context. The most effective product videos follow a simple structure:

1. **Problem** — show the frustration or pain point (5-10 seconds)
2. **Introduction** — present the product as the solution (5 seconds)
3. **Demonstration** — show it working, in use, solving the problem (15-30 seconds)
4. **Result** — show the positive outcome (5-10 seconds)
5. **CTA** — tell them exactly what to do next (5 seconds)

## Tip 6: Add Captions — Always

**85% of Facebook videos are watched without sound.** On Instagram, the number is similar. If your product video relies entirely on audio to communicate its message, you're invisible to the majority of viewers.

Captions aren't just an accessibility feature (though that matters too). They're a conversion feature. Videos with captions see 12% higher watch time and significantly better engagement rates.

Best practices for captions:

- Use large, readable fonts — viewers are on mobile, not cinema screens
- Highlight key words in a different color for emphasis
- Keep captions at the center or lower third of the screen
- Use captions as a design element, not an afterthought

## Tip 7: One Video, One Message

Resist the temptation to cram every selling point into a single video. It's the fastest way to create a confusing, unfocused ad that converts nobody.

**Each product video should deliver one clear message.**

- Video A: "This serum hydrates better than anything I've tried" (benefit-focused)
- Video B: "From dry skin to glowing in 2 weeks" (transformation-focused)
- Video C: "Why 50,000 women switched to this serum" (social proof-focused)

Same product, three different angles, three different audiences. This approach lets you test which message resonates and then double down on the winner. It's infinitely more effective than one bloated video trying to say everything.

## Tip 8: End with an Unmissable CTA

You'd be surprised how many product videos just... end. No call to action. No direction. The viewer enjoyed the video and then scrolls away because nobody told them what to do next.

Your CTA should be:

- **Specific:** "Shop now" beats "Learn more." "Get 20% off today" beats "Visit our website."
- **Urgent:** Give a reason to act now. Limited stock, limited-time offer, seasonal relevance.
- **Visual:** Don't just say it — show it. An end card with a clear button, price, and product image.
- **Verbal + text:** The presenter says the CTA while text reinforces it on screen. Belt and suspenders.

## Tip 10: Optimize for Mobile First

Over 80% of social media consumption happens on mobile. Yet many brands still create videos optimized for desktop viewing. This is a conversion killer.

Mobile-first product video tips:

- **Vertical or square format:** 9:16 for Stories/Reels/TikTok, 1:1 for feed. Never 16:9 for social ads.
- **Large text:** If someone can't read your captions on a phone screen, they don't exist.
- **Close-up shots:** Product details get lost on small screens. Zoom in.
- **Fast pacing:** Mobile viewers have even shorter attention spans. Keep the energy up.
- **Thumb-stopping first frame:** The thumbnail or first frame must pop even at small size in a scrolling feed.

When using AI video creation tools, always preview your output on a phone before publishing. What looks great on a desktop monitor might be unreadable on a 6-inch screen.
